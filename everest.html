Mount Everest: taking India’s environmental message to the roof of the world.

After 41,000+ km of solo cycling across India and summits of regional peaks like Mount Iso and Betlingchhip, Pappu Ram Choudhary is preparing for his most demanding chapter yet — an attempt to summit Mount Everest in 2027 and hoist the Indian tricolour at 8,849 metres.

Photo: Pavel Novak · Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA
Vision

“I want every young Indian’s dream to rise higher than Everest itself.”

Mission Everest 2027 is framed as the summit — literal and symbolic — of Shakti Sankalp Safar. Pappu intends to carry the same message that has guided the cycling mission to the world’s highest point: environmental responsibility, discipline and a drug-free, physically fit India. He treats the pan-India ride itself as a multi-year physical and mental conditioning phase, with every kilometre and every regional peak — from Khardung La to Mount Iso and Betlingchhip — building toward this final ascent.

Training & preparation

From highway kilometres to high-altitude readiness

The Safar itself has doubled as a multi-year physical foundation for the climb ahead.

Ridge & altitude conditioning
Technical terrain exposure
01
2023 – 2024

Endurance base-building

Tens of thousands of kilometres of solo cycling across varied terrain — deserts, coastal plains and Himalayan passes in Jammu & Kashmir and Ladakh — built the aerobic base and mental resilience the climb will demand.

02
High-altitude exposure

Khardung La & Himalayan passes

Riding through some of India’s highest motorable passes provided early exposure to thin-air conditions and cold-weather endurance.

03
Early 2026

Mount Iso & Betlingchhip summits

Technical climbs of Mount Iso (Manipur’s highest peak) and Betlingchhip (Tripura’s highest peak) added real summit experience and rope, ice-axe and crampon familiarity ahead of formal Everest training.

04
Northeast leg

Terrain & weather adaptability

Navigating landslide-prone zones in Sikkim and the hill terrain of Nagaland and Mizoram added further conditioning and risk-management experience.

05
2026 · Pre-expedition phase

Formal mountaineering & technical training

Structured mountaineering courses, altitude acclimatisation protocols and technical climbing skills, undertaken alongside guides and expedition partners ahead of the Everest push.

06
2027

Base Camp & summit push

Final acclimatisation rotations from Everest Base Camp, followed by the summit attempt — carrying the Tricolour to 8,849 metres.
